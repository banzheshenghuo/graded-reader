# The AI Double Hit: A Broken Boom Plus Faster Job Losses

What if the AI bubble breaks, pushing the economy into a sick period — and that sick period makes companies let machines take over jobs even faster? Call it the AI double hit.

The writer has long doubted big claims that AI will change the world. But as the technology races ahead, one worry keeps growing: what it could mean for our jobs.

## First Hit: A Bubble Can Break the Economy

Economic history follows a pattern. In a famous 1978 book about money disasters, the money thinker Charles Kindleberger — building on Hyman Minsky's ideas — looked across centuries and kept seeing the same story. A new technology gets money people excited. They throw money in, then borrow to throw in even more, making the boom bigger. The money they owe becomes a danger: when reality arrives, prices fall but what they owe stays, and a market fall can turn into a crisis and a painful sick period.

The strange part: a technology can be truly world-changing and still end in a fall. The railroads did change the world — and their fall set off the Panic of 1873, which fed an angry people's movement in America.

Today AI is basically holding up the U.S. stock market. One big bank found that just 42 AI companies produced about two thirds of the market's growth since early 2024 — while AI companies borrow hundreds of billions to build data centers and power them.

This doesn't prove a bubble. The first-internet crash of 2000 brought only a light sick period, because companies had borrowed less. But even without a crash there could be pain: the technology watcher Benedict Evans has warned that wild competition between AI companies could drive prices — and profits — down, even as they spend sky-high money on their models.

## Second Hit: Bad Times Speed Up Job Replacement

Here's where studies make the worry stronger. Bad times may be exactly the moment when companies let machines take over people's work.

We imagine robots taking over work as slow and even. But research suggests it happens in jumps — and those jumps come together around bad times. Nir Jaimovich and Henry Siu found that between 1990 and 2017, 88% of the fall in jobs that machines could do happened during — or right around — those bad times.

So picture the double hit: an AI bust hurts the economy, companies rush to cut costs, and the cheap AI tools left behind make it easier than ever to let machines take the jobs. Investors lose money, and workers lose jobs anyway.

## Reasons for Hope

To be fair: nobody knows if AI will earn back the spending, whether a bust would really come, or whether the old pattern will repeat with AI. A sick economy might even leave companies with less money to spend on AI — and AI may end up helping workers get more done rather than taking their jobs.

And there are ways to make any blow lighter: pay for people without work, or — as Bill Gates has suggested — taxes on AI and robots, making it cheaper to keep human workers while paying for a stronger safety net.

Still, writing the worries down felt good — getting them out of the head and onto the page.
