# Visa Tested AI Shopping Agents — and They Failed Nearly 50% of the Time

The study results are on a new Visa website. It shows how 22 AI programs did across 15 shopping trips each. The programs using the newest AI models scored best. Google's Gemini and Meta's version each succeeded 82% of the time. A Claude agent came in third at 76%, while an OpenAI agent finished 6th at 64%.

The chart shows all 22 programs. One line scores how often each program got it right; another shows the cost per shopping job. (Visa notes that the five most expensive programs got it right 67% of the time on average, while the cheapest five only scored 50%.)

The programs' struggle comes from a simple truth: the reasons behind any buy are often complicated, and many shoppers don't know what they want.

Visa's test tried to match this. TV buyers were given a list of needs — the TV had to work with their child's game machine, they wanted a 30-day return window, and they wanted a certain picture quality. Like real shoppers, they only shared some of this information.

So for an AI program to do well, it had to ask the right questions to find out what was missing. The goal was to buy a TV that fit the shopper's needs. Many programs bought the wrong thing, or failed to suggest anything suitable at all.

"We're not there yet from a trust or technology view," said Visa's growth head Rubail Birwadker. "But we'll soon get to a place where people can trust AI and hand over the job."

He noted that AI shopping is still young but moving fast. Finding the right product is just one part of this new type of shopping that stores and AI companies are working on.

For AI shopping to become normal, companies also need safe payment systems — a challenge Visa and its main competitor are already building tools for.

There are also legal questions no one has answered: who pays when an AI program buys the wrong thing or does something dishonest?

He said these questions will get answered over time, just as people learned to trust regular online shopping. But he added that AI shopping will feel very different from how we shop today.

The company also launched a website where people can try the AI programs used in its study.
