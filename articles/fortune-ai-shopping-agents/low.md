# Visa Tested AI Shopping Helpers — and They Failed Nearly Half the Time

A big company ran a test. It gave 22 AI programs a job: buy a TV. The results are now on a website. The best programs, powered by the newest AI models, got it right 82% of the time. But that still means they failed almost 1 in 5 times.

The test showed all 22 programs. One line showed how often each program got it right. Another showed the cost.

## Why Is This Hard?

The problem is simple: people often don't know what to buy.

The test tried to be like real life. TV buyers got a list of needs — it must work with their child's game machine, they want to be able to send it back within 30 days, and they want a certain picture quality. But just like real people, the shopper only shared some of this information.

So for an AI program to do well, it had to ask the right questions. The goal was to buy a TV that fit what they needed. Many programs bought the wrong thing, or failed to suggest anything suitable at all.

## Not Ready Yet

"We're not there yet," said the company's growth head. "But we'll soon get to a place where people can trust AI and hand over the job."

He said AI shopping is still young but moving fast. Finding the right thing to buy is just one part of it.

For AI shopping to become normal, companies also need safe ways to pay. That is still being worked on.

There are also new legal questions: when an AI program buys the wrong thing, who pays? He said these will get answered in time, just as people learned to buy things online.

The company also put up a page where people can try the AI programs.
