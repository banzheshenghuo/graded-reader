Last Thursday, Microsoft Outlook went down. A software bug caused a chain of problems. In some places, workers were sent home because they couldn't get to their email.

Then on Friday, the FAA stopped all flights in the country. Millions of people were stranded. The cause is still being looked into. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the government switched from an old copper wire to new digital fiber, and that caused problems in the network. So they shut down the airspace.

Then on Monday, JPMorgan Chase customers couldn't make transactions. The company said a software error caused it. And on Tuesday, Apple users couldn't use iCloud or Apple Pay after an update went wrong.

So within a few days, a string of tech problems disrupted the lives of millions of Americans. It shows how breakable our old digital systems are. But why is this all happening at once?

We asked Dmitri Alperovitch. He is the former head of security at the company CrowdStrike, and he started a security research institute at Johns Hopkins University.

Is there any evidence that all these incidents are connected, or tied to cybercrime?

No. People are suspicious, and it is strange that these things happen almost every day. But these things happen. One of Murphy's laws is: never think someone is evil when poor work explains it. A lot of this is happening because people are in the middle of upgrading right now. There is, of course, the AI threat. A lot of weak spots are being found by these AI models. As a result, many companies and groups around the world are trying to fix these weak spots as fast as possible. So we are going through a "fixing storm" right now. Sometimes when you update systems, things go wrong, and I think that is partly what is happening here.

So are we seeing a collision of our heavy use of technology, overtaxed and aging systems that need updating? Cyberattacks are common. Rogue AI is a fear. Are we seeing the fragility of the system all coming together?

Well, these are all different systems. There is no question that we rely on technology much more now, so when things go down, it causes much more inconvenience. In the old days, it was easier to walk into your bank and physically withdraw money. Now we rely on online banking, so the impact on us is much greater. But these things have always happened. I do think there is something to the idea that right now it is happening more because everyone is trying to update their systems before they can get hacked by these AI models.

More weak spots have been found this year than in the last 30 years combined. That is a result of AI looking at these software packages and finding new weak spots. So companies and government offices are flooded with reports saying, "You need to update your systems because there is another serious weak spot that has just been found."

Could that mean we are going through a rough patch right now, but it will be temporary? Or is this an ongoing symptom of our modern age?

I think the next 12 to 18 months are going to be very rough, not just because of the update cycles, but also because some people will not update in time. There will be more break-ins as a result. But I think we will come out on the other end in a much safer way, because a lot of the software will all get much more secure.

It sounds like a never-ending game of cat and mouse between technology, upgrades, and bad actors looking for flaws. So does that suggest this is just going to be the ongoing game that lasts forever?

This has been the life of cybersecurity for the last 40 years, and I do not think that is going away. As long as there are people out there with malicious intentions, whether it is nation-states or criminals or hacktivists, we are going to have problems. The biggest weak spot remains the user, not your computer system, because people fall for trick scams. And I expect that to continue to be the case.

People have weak passwords, and they might not use two-step sign-in. What basic things should people be doing to protect themselves?

In terms of your own systems, yes, update it automatically. Make sure you are fully fixed whenever a weak spot comes out. Make sure you have two-step sign-in and strong passwords. But when it comes to this big system going down, we are all watchers here, and unfortunately sometimes victims, because if your bank goes down, there is not a lot you can do.

When you looked at the FAA ground stop, which appears to have been related to an update in Arkansas where something did not go well and suddenly the whole system went down, is there no way to prevent small mistakes in isolated areas from crashing an entire system?

Usually there is. This was a surprise — there is this one weak spot in the system that could take the whole thing down. Usually, there are redundancies. The problem is that a lot of these systems have gotten so complex, with so many layers over the years, that it is very difficult to understand where those points of problem might be. Hopefully this is one area where AI might actually help us to find these problems ahead of time.

Many people are so focused on the negatives of AI and the fear that it will go rogue and cause chaos, but you are saying here is a reminder that it could help us in this situation.

Absolutely. I think it is going to do some amazing things for us in lots of different areas. But there is no question, like every technology, it comes with downsides.

It sounds like your industry has work for infinity, basically.

There is a lot of job security in cybersecurity.

In terms of an overall parting thought, is there any way we as individuals can try to minimize the impact of this on our lives? What is your advice for those who want to throw their hands up in the air?

Everyone should focus on being tough in their daily life. Always have some cash lying around, just in case you cannot get your money out of an ATM for a period of time. Stockpile some water. Stockpile some food. I think those are smart things to do in any case. Of course, there could be other big disasters — weather-related, terrorism, etc. — that you want to be ready for. So that is always a good thing to do.
