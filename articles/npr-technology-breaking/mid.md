Last Thursday, Microsoft Outlook went down. A software bug caused a chain of failures. In some places, workers were sent home because they could not get to their email.

Then on Friday, the FAA stopped all flights in the country. Millions of people were stranded. The cause is still being looked into. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the government switched from an old copper wire to new digital fiber, and that caused problems in the network. So they shut down the airspace.

Then on Monday, JPMorgan Chase customers could not make transactions. The company said a software bug caused it. And on Tuesday, Apple users could not use iCloud or Apple Pay after an update went wrong.

So within a few days, a string of tech failures disrupted the lives of millions of Americans. It shows how fragile our old digital systems are. But why is this all happening at once?

We asked Dmitri Alperovitch. He is the former chief technology officer of the security company CrowdStrike, and he founded a security institute at Johns Hopkins University.

Is there any evidence that all these incidents are connected, or tied to cybercrime?

No. People are suspicious, and it is strange that these things happen almost every day. But these things happen. One of Murphy's laws is: never attribute anything to malice that is adequately explained by incompetence. I think a lot of this is happening because people are in the middle of upgrading right now. There is, of course, the AI threat. A lot of weak spots are being found by these AI models. As a result, many companies and groups around the world are trying to fix these weak spots as quickly as possible. So we are going through this fixing storm right now. Sometimes when you are updating systems, we all know things can go wrong, and I think that is partly what is happening here.

So are we seeing a collision of our heavy use of technology, overtaxed and aging systems that need updating? Cyberattacks are common. Rogue AI is a fear. Are we seeing the fragility of the system all coming together?

Well, these are all different systems. There is no question that we rely on technology much more now, so when things go down, it causes much more inconvenience. In the old days, it was easier to walk into your bank and physically withdraw money. Now we rely on online banking, so the impact on us is much greater. But these things have always happened. I do think there is something to the idea that right now it is happening more because everyone is trying to update their systems before they can get hacked by these AI models.

I was going to say updates are a fact of life, but you are saying particular updates happening now because of the risks of AI that are becoming apparent — people trying to get ahead of that.

That is right. More weak spots have been found this year than in the last 30 years combined. That is a result of AI looking at these software packages and finding new weak spots. So companies and government offices are flooded with reports that you need to update your systems because there is another serious weak spot that has just been found.

Could that mean we are going through a rough patch right now, but it will be temporary? Or is this going to be an ongoing symptom of our modern age?

I think the next 12 to 18 months are going to be very rough, not just because of the update cycles, but also because some people will not upgrade in time. There will be more hacks as a result. But I think we will come out on the other end in a much safer way, because a lot of the software will get much more secure.

It sounds like your industry has work for infinity, basically.

There is a lot of job security in security.

In terms of an overall parting thought, is there any way we as individuals can try to minimize the impact of this on our lives? What is your advice for those who want to throw their hands up in the air?

Everyone should focus on resilience in their daily life. Always have some cash lying around, just in case you cannot get your money withdrawn from an ATM for a period of time. Stockpile some water. Stockpile some groceries. I think those are prudent things to do in any scenario. Of course, there could be other catastrophes — weather-related, terrorism, etc. — that you want to be prepared for. So that is always a good thing to do.
