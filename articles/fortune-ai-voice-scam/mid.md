# Scammers Used AI to Clone Her Daughter's Voice and Fake a Kidnapping

Criminals can now lift voice samples from videos people casually post on Instagram and TikTok. They use the copies to convince family members that a loved one is in danger — and talk them out of thousands of dollars.

## Five Hours of Fear

One morning in May, Deborah Del Mastro, a mother in Martinez, California, got a call from an unknown number. The man claimed a Mexican drug group had taken her 37-year-old daughter. Then he played what sounded like her daughter crying for help.

"It was my daughter's voice having a total panic attack, scared, telling me 'I love you, mom, I'm so sorry, I'm so scared' — and then they just cut it off," Del Mastro told ABC7 News. The man kept shouting orders: don't speak, get dressed, get out, do it now.

Believing her daughter's life was on the line, Del Mastro followed his instructions for five hours. She sent $5,400 to Mexico from several places, and was told her daughter would be freed at a grocery store. When her daughter wasn't there, she called her directly — her daughter had been at work the whole time. Police are investigating, but Del Mastro doesn't expect to get the money back.

## Why the Brain Falls for It

Kimberly Hyun, a researcher who studies how a person's voice shapes what we believe, told Fortune these tricks work because of how the brain processes sound: when a voice sounds like someone we know, we drop our defenses. A family member's voice "can actually trick you through trust."

It's not only older people — one study found no difference in victims by age. In 2024, a finance worker in Hong Kong paid out about $25.6 million after a deepfake video call where every "colleague" was fake, police said.

## The Tools Are Easy to Find

Consumer Reports tested six popular AI voice-copying products this year. Four of them — including the well-known ElevenLabs and Speechify — let researchers easily copy a voice from public audio, with nothing more than a checkbox saying the user had the right to do it. Only two products had taken real steps against secret copying.

"There are basic steps companies can take," said policy analyst Grace Gedye, "but some companies aren't taking them." Researchers warn the fakes have now crossed the line where they can't be told apart from real voices.

## The Defense: A Code Word

Since a familiar voice is no longer proof of identity, you need a second check. Hyun's advice: agree on a code word with your loved ones — something only you two know. If a caller claims a family member is in trouble, don't stay on the line; hang up and call that family member yourself. And if it happens to you, report it — most victims never do.
