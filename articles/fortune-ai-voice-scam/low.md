# AI Copied Her Daughter's Voice to Steal $5,400

Bad people can now take a person's voice from short videos online — like the ones people post every day. Then they use that copied voice to trick families into sending money.

## The Phone Call

One morning in May, Deborah Del Mastro, a mother in California, got a call from a number she didn't know. The man said her 37-year-old daughter had been taken by a bad group in Mexico. Then she heard something horrible: her daughter's own voice, crying for help.

"It was my daughter's voice, totally afraid, telling me 'I love you, mom, I'm so sorry' — and then they just cut it off," Del Mastro told ABC7 News.

The man on the phone kept shouting orders: don't speak, get dressed, get out, do it now. Believing her daughter's life was in danger, Del Mastro did what he said for five long hours. She sent $5,400 to Mexico from different places. Then she went to a food store to pick up her daughter — who wasn't there. She called her daughter's number. Her daughter had been at work the whole time.

The police are looking into it, but Del Mastro doesn't expect to get her money back.

## Why It Works

A researcher who studies how voices change what we believe said: when a voice sounds like someone we love, we stop being careful. A family member's voice "can trick you through your own trust."

And it's not just older people. One study found no difference between ages. In 2024, a money worker in Hong Kong sent about $25 million to cheats who used a fake video call — every "colleague" on it was fake.

## How to Stay Safe

The fake voices are getting better fast. So a familiar voice is no longer proof of who is calling. You need a second way to check.

One simple idea: agree on a secret word with your family — a word only you two know. And if someone calls saying a family member is in trouble, put the phone down, then call that family member yourself. That simple step can stop the whole plan.
