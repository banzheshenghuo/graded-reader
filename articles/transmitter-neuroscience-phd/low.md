Diane Lipscombe teaches brain science at Brown University. For many years she has helped young people start a career in science. She says they are an important part of university work.

But this is a hard time for students in the United States. The government has cut a lot of money for science. So many schools are taking fewer new students. Teachers are also not sure they can pay for a student for all the years of a Ph.D.

The Transmitter asked her some questions.

**Is this a good time to think again about why we have a Ph.D.?**

Yes. New scientists are clever and full of new ideas. Every new group is better than the last. It would be very sad to stop new people from coming in.

Who should take care of this? Everyone. Schools must give students time to learn one subject well. And the people who pay for science must help too. The best science needs young scientists with new ideas. I see no other way.

**What changes do you see?**

One big change is AI. Students must learn how to use it and when not to use it. The way you ask a question decides the answer. It is like before, but the fine parts matter more now.

The second change is joining small and big ideas. In brain science it is hard to go from tiny cells to the whole mind. I hope new ways will help. The human genome project did this well, and we all want to do the same.

**What worries you most?**

First, how long a Ph.D. takes. We say five years, but many students need more. Sometimes there are good reasons. But a student should not work for six or seven years without a clear goal. There must be a plan to take care of them.

Second, some schools now want students to get a higher degree first. In the U.S. you pay for that degree yourself, so poor students cannot do it. That would shut the door on many good people. It would be a big step back. In the past, the government paid for most Ph.D. students.

**Fewer students come from other countries. Is that bad?**

Yes, very bad. Applications from other countries fell by about 40 percent from 2025 to 2026. This is a danger to science in the U.S. Many of us feel we can do nothing.

**You like the T32 program. Why?**

T32 is a government program that pays and trains students. It costs about $50,000 per student each year. That is not much for what it gives.

It helps the whole school, not only the students in it. Students can work in two or three labs. A group of teachers, not just one, watches each student. They meet and talk often. So good ideas spread to other schools too.

The bad part: only U.S. citizens and people who live there for good can get T32 money.

**Are students losing interest in a Ph.D.?**

Many feel sad and worn out. When money for science is cut, students cannot see their future. If one road is clear and the other is not, they will ask hard questions. That is a big risk now.

**What do you say to new students?**

Keep going after what you love. Brain science is the most exciting field, and it can help people live better. Money is a worry now, but in the past it always came back. Stick to your dream.
