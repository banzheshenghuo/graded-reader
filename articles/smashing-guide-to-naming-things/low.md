Naming things is hard. Words change how we think. They also change how we talk with other people. That is why we get confused about colors, icons, parts of a page, and features. It is also why picking names for our code is hard.

Some names are too general. Then other people do not know what we mean. Some names are too special. Then we cannot use them again in a new place. So how do we pick good names?

These links can help. They are full of word lists for naming.

The first one is Classnames. It gives lists of words in groups. Some words are easy to think of. Some are not. You can find words about nature, art, theater, music, buildings, fashion, and books.

The second one is a list of color names. David Aerne keeps it, and it has more than 30,000 names. It also has a tool to pick colors and to search for a name.

So what makes a good name? Javier Cuello wrote a short guide. A good name is short. It has a clear meaning. Everyone knows it. And it does not talk about how the thing looks. He also shows what to do and what not to do.

Intuit has many products, like Mailchimp and TurboTax. Its team had to name its color and style values for every product. Nate Baldwin wrote about how they did it. He tells you what was hard, what rules they used, and how they built the new system.

Another way to find a name is to look at other teams. What do they call the same thing? Iain Bean made the Component Gallery for this. It shows more than 50 page parts, such as the accordion, and also lists the other names for each one.

Sometimes people do not use a feature. This happens when they cannot find it. First they must find it. Then they must understand it, try it, and learn it. Erin Gannon wrote a guide about this. Good names come from what users need. And they should use the user's own words. So ask people to say what a feature does in their own words. Then use their words.

Vodafone UK made a map of its names. The map shows four groups and how they are joined. It helps people see where a name is used and what it is for, just from the name.

Romina Kavcic made a small tool for naming. You can learn how names work and build your own naming plan.

Are you naming a product? Onym is a free list of tools and guides. It helps you find names, check names, learn where words come from, and read about names that failed.

So a name is right when your team and your users both know it. We lose a lot of time when we talk about the same thing in different words. Maybe the designer, the developer, the manager, and the user all use a different word. This brings bad feeling and noise. Sometimes a team does not use a new feature just because of its name. So watch for names that do not work. Write them down and fix them later. It is a small thing, but it helps teams work better.
