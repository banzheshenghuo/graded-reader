# The Death of the Button: Why the Best Interface Is No Interface

For over forty years, software has depended on point-and-click screens. If you wanted to book a trip, buy shoes, or check a health symptom, you had to find your way through many menus, long forms, and pop-ups. We grew used to spending more time managing the software than reaching our goals. That deal is now changing. Thanks to AI, a new generation of web tools is built around human intent — what you want — instead of buttons.

## The "Ten-Click" Problem

Think about buying a flight online. The experience is made for endless clicking: you type your cities, pick dates from a little window, set filters, choose seats, and enter payment details across several pages. Want to compare airlines? Open a new page and start over. The computer sits there like a box of data that does nothing, and the human does all the work.

## Three Examples of Intent-Driven Design

This new design turns that around: you state a goal — "find me a direct flight to Chicago next weekend under $400" — and the system does the searching, comparing, and booking steps for you.

1. **Perplexity AI: search without open tabs.** Old search engines were lists of links; you opened ten tabs to check one fact. Perplexity answers the whole question at once.
2. **Vercel v0: web design without dragging boxes.** For years, building a site meant pulling elements around in a design tool. v0 builds a working page from a plain-English request.
3. **Goblin.tools: turning big tasks into small steps.** It takes "prepare dinner for guests" and breaks it into a clear, calm list.

## The Three Pillars of This New Design

1. **High-level goals.** You express what you want, not how to get it.
2. **Generative pages.** The page you see is built for your question, not made the same for everyone.
3. **AI helpers doing the work** — entering forms, comparing prices, writing first versions.

## The Hidden Risks

Taking away menus and forms feels freeing, but it creates new dangers.

**The feeling of control.** When a site decides for you, it can quickly feel like it looks down on you — as if the software knows better. Good design keeps the human in charge: show what the system is about to do, and make it easy to say no.

**The closed-box problem.** In a traditional UI, when you get a strange result, you can usually find the cause: a wrong filter, a missed field. With an AI-driven system, you cannot see inside the machine's reasoning. Designers must show the steps the system took, not only the final answer — or users will lose faith in it.

## What Designers Should Learn Now

Web design is not dying; it is moving. Instead of drawing buttons and forms, designers will shape how machines ask questions, show their work, and step back. Success will no longer be measured by how beautiful a site looks, but by how little of it you need to see. The best screen may soon be no screen at all.
