# More Code, More Work? A Debate About AI and Coding

Last week I talked with Claire Giordano on a podcast. The subject: how AI is changing the work of building software. Here are two ideas from our talk.

**Idea 1: counting lines of code.** Many people say you should not measure your work by counting lines of code. I do not fully agree. In the old days, a good engineer wrote 200 lines of good, working code in a day — and that was a great day. Most days it was 50 or 60. With AI tools, you can now make a thousand lines in a day. That is a real improvement — but only if the code is still good: easy to change, well tested. Getting there takes a lot of skill.

Here is the interesting part. If one person can work a hundred times faster, why have a team at all? Because of the size of a human head. I can make code very fast, but I cannot hold a hundred times more of it in my mind. So we still need teams — a team shares the thinking.

**Idea 2: software that fits together.** There is an old idea about good software: everything fits together well, and there are no surprises. With AI tools, that is much harder. You get an idea, you type a request, and five minutes later the new part exists. The software starts to grow in strange directions, like a plant with no gardener.

Claire told a story about this. There is a famous house in America with 140 rooms. The woman who built it kept adding rooms for 40 years, because she was afraid of ghosts. Why? Building a new room was cheap for her. And that is exactly the problem with AI tools: adding a new part is now so cheap that we do it too often. In the end, the software stops making sense as one thing.

Before, if I had a crazy idea, I would think: "that would take me a week — forget it." Now it takes an hour, so it is much easier to say yes. The old limits are gone. What you need now is discipline.
