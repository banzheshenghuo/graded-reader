# How AI Is Transforming Hurricane Forecasts

Hurricane forecasters are using AI more and more to make their predictions — and they say the technology is quickly changing their field.

When the first hurricane of the Atlantic season formed this month, an AI program predicted where it would go better than the older programs did. In early October, Hurricane Isaias hit Florida as a Category 2 storm. Older programs first said it would head toward Louisiana — but Google DeepMind's AI program called it right from the start: western Florida.

"Google DeepMind has really proven itself as the gold standard," said Michael Lowry, who has studied storms for many years. "It has been, by far and away, the best performing program we've ever seen. Period."

The National Hurricane Center started using the DeepMind program in its official reports last year. By the end of the 2025 season it was already one of the best at predicting a storm's path. "The new kid on the block very quickly became the best one out there," said storm researcher Brian McNoldy. "That's impressive."

## Two Ways to Predict

Older physics-based programs take live readings from the air — temperature, pressure, wind — and use complex math to predict a storm's path and strength.

AI works differently: it studies thousands of past storms and finds patterns, without using the physics of the air at all. "The AI programs don't really know anything about the air beyond the current picture," Lowry said. Most of the people building them aren't even weather experts — they're software makers building brain-like computer programs.

The result: AI can seem like a "black box." "We don't know why it gets a forecast right. We also don't know why it gets one wrong," Lowry said. That can feel worrying when millions depend on the answer. "We like to know why you're getting the right answer," McNoldy said. "And I don't know why."

## Humans Still Decide

Not all AI programs have done well. The two most trusted are Google DeepMind and the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts. The Hurricane Center uses several proven programs — AI and older ones — and mixes them. "Every storm has different challenges. That's why we take that mix-and-match approach," said center director Michael Brennan.

And people still read the results. The programs can show big changes between updates; forecasters turn those shifts into clear advice people can act on. "That's what the human forecaster provides: steady guidance," Brennan said. "We turn that forecast into real-world dangers, and that's what people need to watch for."

The DeepMind program follows storm paths well but is weaker at guessing strength. Brennan's team sends regular notes to Google to help make it better.

## Getting Better Fast

Forecasting has always been changing, Brennan said. Errors in strength forecasts are about half what they were when Hurricane Katrina hit. But AI updates come in weeks or months, not years. "They are making progress in one year that would have taken us ten," Lowry said.

Yet he is not worried about AI replacing people. "I don't feel some deep fear about AI. If anything, it helps our work."
