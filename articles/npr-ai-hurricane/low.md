# How AI Is Changing Hurricane Forecasts

People who predict hurricanes are now using AI — and they say it's changing everything about their work.

When the first storm of the season formed this year, an AI program knew where it would go before the old computer programs did.

## The New Model Wins

In early October, Hurricane Isaias hit Florida as a Category 2 storm. The old forecasting models said it would go further west, toward Louisiana. But Google DeepMind's AI model called it right from the start: the storm was going to hit western Florida.

"Google DeepMind has proven itself the best program we've ever seen. Period," said Michael Lowry, who has studied storms for many years.

The National Hurricane Center started using the DeepMind program in its official reports last year. Usually new programs take years to get better. "This one came out in first place," said storm researcher Brian McNoldy.

## Two Very Different Ways to Predict

Old programs use real numbers from the sky — how hot, how heavy the air, how hard the wind blows — and use math to guess what a storm will do.

AI works differently. It studies thousands of past storms and finds patterns. It knows nothing about how the air around the Earth really works.

"The people building these programs aren't even weather experts," Lowry said. "They're software makers building brain-like computer programs."

This means AI can feel like a closed box: nobody knows why it gives the right or wrong answer. That can feel worrying when millions of people depend on the answer.

## Humans Still Decide

The Hurricane Center uses several trusted programs — AI and older ones — and mixes their results. People still decide what to tell the public.

"Every storm has different challenges," said center director Michael Brennan. "That's why we use the best of both."

The DeepMind program follows where storms go very well, but it is less good at guessing how strong they will get. His team sends notes to Google to help make it better.

## Getting Better Fast

The exciting thing about AI is how quickly it gets better. Old programs update once a year. AI programs update in weeks or months.

"They are making more progress in one year than we used to make in ten," Lowry said. But he is not worried about AI taking people's jobs.

"I don't feel some deep fear about AI," he said. "If anything, it helps our work."
