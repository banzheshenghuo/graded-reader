# The AI Rich: Rich Fast, and Feeling Alone

In the 1990s, two mind doctors in California gave a name to a new problem: people getting rich very fast from the internet, faster than their hearts could handle. Now, they say, the same trouble is back — this time because of AI.

## The New Rich Feel Alone

"Everyone coming out of the AI money wave is getting more money than ever," said Joan DiFuria, one of the two mind doctors. "And feeling more mixed feelings about it, too."

There is now a whole industry to help. One club for the very rich meets for a full day each month — but you need at least $20 million to join. ASwiss hospital for the mind gives rich patients a private cook, a driver and a doctor who lives with them. It costs about $131,000 a week.

## Why This Wave Feels Different

Here is the strange part: many of these new rich feel guilty, because their money comes from a technology that many people fear. Even the head of a top AI company has warned that AI could take away half of all easy office jobs.

"If you're already worried about your wealth, and the whole world is saying, 'What you're doing will kill me or take my job' — how do you talk to your friends?" DiFuria said.

The numbers are huge: hundreds of AI companies are now worth more than a billion dollars each. About 75 people from one AI company alone walked away with $30 million each in a single sale.

## Houses Behind Walls

Many of the new rich feel cut off from normal life. The very rich live in walled areas by lakes and oceans. One famous tech boss built a huge house with a room under the ground. Another spent about $237 million on three houses on a private island behind gates.

One therapist called this "a major social problem": the rich live up on the hill, far from everyone below. Some rich children almost never leave the house — "they don't know what a poor person looks like."

## What Helps

The two therapists try to push their clients the other way. In first meetings, they ask them to write down their values and give some money away. But they admit: the people who need help most rarely ask for it.

One family moved to a place full of other billionaires. "We found our people," they said. "We don't have to hide that we have a plane, a boat and five homes."
