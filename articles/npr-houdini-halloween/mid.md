# 100 Years Later, Houdini's Death Still Haunts Detroit

This Halloween marks 100 years since the death of Harry Houdini, the greatest escape man of all time.

He earned his name by escaping from anything: handcuffs, jail cells, locked boxes full of water. But he could not escape the fate waiting for him in Detroit on Oct. 31, 1926 — and his ghost, people say, still walks the city.

## From Unknown to Star

Houdini played in Detroit 19 times between 1894 and his last show in 1926, rising from the bottom of the show list to world fame.

He said no chains or locked room could hold him. He hung head-down from buildings in a special jacket, getting free while crowds watched below — and the buildings were often newspaper offices, so the next day's paper gave him free ads.

In 1906, in Detroit, he jumped about seven metres from a bridge into the icy river, tied in police handcuffs. He came out safe — but the legend grew that he had jumped through a hole in the ice, finding air under the water. The river wasn't really frozen. Movies showed it that way anyway.

As other magicians copied his acts, Houdini raised the risk. His most famous trick, first shown in 1914, was the Water Torture Cell: a water box the size of a coffin. He was dropped in head first, feet locked at the top, a curtain hiding his method.

Even Dorothy Young, his stage helper at 17, got goosebumps watching it. "Mrs. Houdini always stood in the wings because they had a secret code in case he couldn't get out," she said in 2004. She was the last person alive who had worked with him.

## The Punch

The real cause traces back to Montreal, just before Detroit. Houdini often said proudly that he could survive any hit to the stomach. In his dressing room, a college wrestler took the challenge and hit him several times before Houdini could make his stomach hard.

He was in great pain but would not stop his shows — he had made a promise. He finished one performance, falling between acts, and had to be carried back to his hotel. At the hospital, doctors found his appendix had burst. Days and two operations later, on Halloween, he died.

The theater, the hotel, even the hospital are gone now. The funeral home that prepared his body became a girls' art school — where some students would not enter the kitchen. "I might see Houdini," they said.

## The Final Goodbye

His wife Bess spent years at meetings trying to contact the dead, searching for his ghost. The strange part: Houdini himself had spent his final years showing everyone that mind readers were liars, taking money from sad families.

At a meeting on Halloween, ten years after his death, Bess finally said goodbye. "You remember what he always said? That it was impossible for the dead to return," she said. "This proves he was right."

People still try to reach him. At the American Museum of Magic near Detroit sits Houdini's famous milk can — he once escaped from it underwater, locked with six locks. This Halloween, a researcher will hold another séance by the can — and place a lox and bagel sandwich inside, inviting Houdini's ghost to take a bite. Dying on Halloween was the best show of his whole life: the story has only grown for a hundred years.
