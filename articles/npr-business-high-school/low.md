# Business as a School Subject? More High Schools Say Yes

At a high school near Birmingham, Alabama, students sit in a half circle and talk about money. The question: what to do with the pay from their part-time jobs?

"I put mine in big shared funds," one student says. Another saves most of his pay in an account for when he is old. A third buys small pieces of companies. Their teacher laughs: "Oh, you watch the markets? Love it."

## A New Kind of Class

These students are among the first in the country to take a new class: AP Business with Personal Finance. It opened this year at more than 1,500 high schools across America.

AP classes are college-level courses for high school students. Usually those courses are older subjects — English, history, chemistry. This new one teaches business and money instead: how companies work, and the outside forces that can lift a business up or sink it — politics, money matters, technology, even laws.

"Business means always learning to change," says the head of the organization that created the course. His group added it because students said they want to turn their ideas into real jobs.

## A Growing Wave

The class is part of a bigger change in American schools. This year, 39 states require a class about money before students can finish high school — four more states than two years ago.

Does teaching money in school actually work? A study from the country's central bank found that families in states with these classes made better money choices. In the hard years of the sickness wave, they used their government payments to pay off credit cards instead of spending them.

"When we teach young people to use money in a smart way, they're really good at it," says one education professor.

## "There's a Business in Everything"

The students see it in daily life too. One girl in the class says her father ran a trucking company and her brother studies economics — so business talk follows her to the dinner table.

"There's a business in everything," she says. "If you want to understand how it works, it really opens up the opportunities to a person."
