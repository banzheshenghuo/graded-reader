# Ukraine and the U.S. Are at Odds Over Starshield. What to Know

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and U.S. officials remain at odds over breaks in service to a key space-based communication network used by Ukraine's army.

Zelenskyy told NBC's Meet the Press on Sunday he believes the U.S. government is behind his army's loss of normal access to the Starshield and Starlink services, claiming tech company SpaceX put the restrictions in place after a "message from [the] American government."

Warning that the U.S. siding with Russian President Vladimir Putin would be a "historic mistake," the Ukrainian leader claimed that the Starshield system has not fully come back, no matter what U.S. officials have said in public.

In a post on X over the weekend, Zelenskyy said the restrictions have hurt Ukraine's power to intercept weapons fired by Russia, calling the consequences "very painful and unpleasant."

## What Is Starshield?

Starlink is a space internet network run by Elon Musk's SpaceX. Since 2024, the U.S. Defense Department has shared a high-powered army version of the network — Starshield — with Ukraine, which has cost the Pentagon billions of dollars.

Ukraine leans on the network to defend itself in Russia's nearly five-year war.

"We use Starlink to intercept the jet fighters that are hitting our civilians and killing families," said Zelenskyy online. "We also use Starlink for middle strikes on enemy military targets in our temporarily occupied territories. We were unable to do this when there were restrictions. And the third, most important thing is people. Our soldiers, who have been on the front line for weeks and months, are being delivered food and water using appropriate drones with Starlink."

## Who Is Responsible?

Zelenskyy said the breaks in service came without warning last week, and that the network's speed hasn't fully returned. It is not clear why Ukraine's access was limited. Musk said on X that the U.S. government is responsible for the system, and that "any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and, in the end, POTUS."

A group of lawmakers from both parties is asking the Defense Department's inspector general to look into how the system was disrupted.

President Trump denied interfering: "I don't know if it was anybody because it wasn't me, and I don't think it was Elon. And from what I understand, it's the same as it's always been," he told reporters on Friday.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio also rejected Zelenskyy's claim that the slowdowns were done on purpose. "It's just categorically false. No one at the White House ordered anyone to slow down internet or slow down speed so that certain drones couldn't use it," Rubio said on Saturday — adding that Ukraine requested access to carry out strikes deep inside Russia, a request that is "under discussion."

## A Relationship Under Strain

The quarrel has raised again the question of how far America will go in standing behind Ukraine — Trump and Zelenskyy have been at each other for days. Earlier this month, Trump moved to ease U.S. sanctions against Russia passed by Congress. After Zelenskyy called that a "weak decision by strong partners," Trump hit back, angry about Ukraine hitting Russian oil-making plants — which, he said, was raising U.S. fuel prices — and said: "I think it's time for Ukraine to get a new president."

Zelenskyy also said U.S. intelligence sharing about Russian flying-bomb attacks was blocked for three days, but has started again.

Retired U.S. Navy leader Mark Montgomery told NPR he believes the breaks in service are a signal from the White House: stop attacking oil-making plants. Ukraine, he added, is at an extremely dangerous moment, as more Russian drones get through. Russia fired more than 4,000 attack drones, bombs and missiles over the past week; Saturday night attacks on power stations left tens of thousands in the dark.

Ukraine has answered with its own hits on Russian refineries — and on Sunday, Ukrainian attacks set an oil shipping center on fire in St. Petersburg, as Kyiv sent more than 1,000 drones at Russia.
