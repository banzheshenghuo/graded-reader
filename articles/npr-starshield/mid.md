# Ukraine and the U.S. Are at Odds Over Starshield. What to Know

Ukraine's President Zelenskyy and U.S. officials remain in serious disagreement over a space-based internet service that the Ukrainian army needs for its war.

## What Happened?

Zelenskyy says his army suddenly lost normal service last week, without warning. He believes the U.S. government was behind it, and that SpaceX — the space company of Elon Musk — imposed the limits after a "message from the American government." He called the results "very painful and unpleasant." The White House denies everything.

## What Is Starshield?

Starlink is a space internet network built by Musk's SpaceX. Starshield is its stronger version for the army. Since 2024, the U.S. Defense Department has shared it with Ukraine, at a cost of billions of dollars. For a country fighting Russia's nearly five-year attack, it is a key tool.

Zelenskyy listed its uses: stopping enemy warplanes that hit cities, hitting far-away targets, and bringing food and water to soldiers at the front line by pilotless aircraft.

## Who Is Telling the Truth?

It is not clear why the service was slowed. Musk says the U.S. government controls the system, and any limits are decided by the army and the president. Trump said he had nothing to do with it: "It wasn't me, and I don't think it was Elon." Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Zelenskyy's claims simply false — though he added that Ukraine has asked to use the system for hits deep inside Russia, and that request is "under discussion."

A group of lawmakers from both parties wants an official inquiry into what happened.

## A Relationship Getting Worse

The quarrel comes at a thin-ice moment. This month Trump lifted some punishments on Russia; Zelenskyy called that a "weak decision by strong partners." Trump then spoke angrily about Ukraine hitting Russian oil-making plants — which, he said, was raising fuel prices in America — and suggested Ukraine needs a new president.

Zelenskyy also said U.S. warnings about Russian flying-bomb attacks were blocked for three days, though they have started again.

Meanwhile the war continues: Russia fired more than 4,000 pilotless aircraft and flying bombs at Ukraine this past week, and Ukraine answered with hits on Russian oil targets — including more than 1,000 of its own aircraft sent at Russia in one night.
