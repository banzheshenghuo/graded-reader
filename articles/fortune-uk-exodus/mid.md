# Not Just Billionaires: a Record 69,300 Workers Have Left the U.K.

Last year, about 69,300 people filed the special form that tells the UK tax office you are leaving the country for good — a third more than the year before, and a record. The real number is probably higher, because many people who leave never file it.

There has been a well-known wave of rich residents quitting Britain. But new reporting suggests that for ordinary workers, tax is not the main reason — life is.

## It Is Not the Money, a Job Coach Says — It Is Sunshine

The UK government has raised a series of taxes in recent years, making family costs heavier. So when people leave, everyone supposes tax is the reason.

But Natasha Stanley, who leads coaching at the careers site Careershifters and has helped thousands of people change direction, told Fortune she cannot think of a single client who left for tax reasons.

So what pulls people abroad? Her answer: the life on offer. "Lots of people are moving for lifestyle purposes and more sunshine," she said.

Some move to join partners from other countries. Others watch the NHS struggle and look for countries where public services run better. But most, she says, are simply tired after years of grey weather — and many have moved happily to Spain and Switzerland. "Others just think, I'm done with the rat race here, and if I'm going to change career, I might as well live somewhere with the lifestyle I want."

## The Research Backs Her Up

Spain just topped a major survey of life abroad for the fifth year running. One in five people living abroad there said a better quality of life was their main reason for moving; 89% are happy with the climate, and 83% with the healthcare. Jobs are not the pull — Spain ranks near the bottom for working abroad.

Even among the very rich, tax is not the only reason. A company that advises rich movers lists safety, climate, and healthcare among the common reasons, and its 2024 report pointed to the UK's getting-worse health system and rising safety worries in cities like London.

Billionaire Lakshmi Mittal has just left Britain after three decades for Switzerland — now also home to other very rich people.
