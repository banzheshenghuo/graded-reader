# Not Just Billionaires: a Record 69,300 Workers Have Left the U.K.

Around 69,300 people filed the form that tells the UK tax office you are leaving the country for good in the last tax year — up a third from the year before, and a record. The true number is likely higher, since many people who leave never file it.

It's no secret that there's been a wave of wealthy residents quitting Britain. But saving money takes most of the blame — and one career coach says it is actually sunshine and lifestyle.

## Tax Is the Usual Explanation. A Coach Disagrees

Recent years have brought higher taxes on homes, private schools, and the money you make when you sell things — making life dearer for households. So when people talk about the wave of leavers, the idea behind it is always money.

But Natasha Stanley, head of coaching at the careers site Careershifters, has spent well over a decade helping thousands of people change careers — and she says she can't think of a single client who has left for tax reasons. "For tax purposes specifically, I can't think of anyone," she told Fortune.

So if it isn't tax pulling people abroad, what is it? According to Stanley, it's the life on offer. "Lots of people are moving for lifestyle purposes and more sunshine," she said.

Some move to be with partners from other countries. Others are watching the NHS — Britain's National Health Service — crumble and looking for countries where public services work better. But most people have simply had enough of grinding through the grey weather; she has seen professionals happily relocating to Spain and Switzerland. "Others just think, I'm done with the rat race here, and if I'm going to change career, I might as well take this opportunity to live somewhere that offers me the kind of lifestyle that I'm looking for," Stanley added.

## Research Shows She Has a Point

Spain has just topped a big quality-of-life survey of people living abroad for the fifth year running. One in five expats there say a better life was their main reason for moving; around 89% are happy with the climate, and 83% with the healthcare. And it certainly isn't the job market pulling them in: Spain ranks near the bottom of the same survey for working abroad.

Even among the wealthy, tax isn't the only pull. A company that advises wealthy clients on relocating lists safety, climate, and healthcare among the common reasons millionaires move, and its 2024 report called the UK's health system worse each year and pointed to growing safety worries in cities like London.

And much like the ordinary movers Stanley sees, the very rich are also eyeing up Switzerland — where the views look like a postcard. Steel billionaire Lakshmi Mittal has just left Britain after three decades for the Alps, now also home to other billionaire residents.
