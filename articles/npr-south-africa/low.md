# South Africa: 'My whole country feels like a prison'

Elizabeth Moselakgomo was a 38-year-old manager and a mother. She loved running near her home in Johannesburg, South Africa's biggest city.

Last week she went out for her usual afternoon run — and never came home. Her 8-year-old daughter called family, and the family called the police. Five days later, police found her body. Someone had hit her face very hard.

Police say nine women were killed in the same area in a few weeks. Many of them were sexually attacked too.

South Africa already has one of the highest rates of men killing and hurting women in the world. Police statistics say about 15 women are killed every day.

People are surprised, angry and afraid. Police told women: do not walk or run alone. Many women are angry at this. They say: the police must protect us — we should not have to change our lives.

President Cyril Ramaphosa says the police will work hard to find the killer or killers.

Women marched in the streets of Johannesburg and Cape Town to protest. There were also runs in many cities to remember Elizabeth and the other women. Men and women wore black and stood quiet for one minute before they ran.

One runner, 26-year-old Shari Maluluke, told NPR: "You go for a run — you can be killed. You go to the post office — you can be killed. Even in your own home — you can be killed. I cannot go where I want. My whole country feels like a prison."
