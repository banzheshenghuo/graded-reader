On Friday, all flights in the U.S. stopped for about four hours. It was one of the biggest problems in U.S. air travel since September 11, 2001. On Friday night, the FAA said the stop had ended.

Thousands of planes stayed on the ground at every U.S. airport.

Sean Duffy is the U.S. transport chief. At a news meeting on Friday night, he said the radio and screen systems that guide planes had stopped working off and on from the afternoon.

Duffy said old machines were the cause. "We have old switches, old boxes and little metal wires from the 1980s," he said. "It's an old system that went down and went down everywhere. It was too full."

But reporters asked him why the system would fail on a normal day. He had no clear answer.

Markwayne Mullin, the U.S. security chief, also spoke. He said there was no sign of an attack. "We have seen zero evidence that someone broke into our systems," he said.

By 9 p.m., more than 8,000 flights were late on Friday and 7,000 did not fly at all, a flight tracking site called FlightAware said. On Thursday there were already almost 7,000 late flights.

The FAA said the problem was in the local centers that guide planes flying high across the country. FAA leaders did not speak at the news meeting. They gave no more details.

This was only the second time since 9/11 that the whole country stopped flying. The last time was in 2023. Then a system called NOTAM, which tells pilots about danger, did not work. People feared a cyber attack. Later the FAA found that workers had deleted important files by mistake.

Richard Blumenthal, a U.S. lawmaker from Connecticut, asked for a fast and deep study of the causes. He said, "A big radio stoppage like this is very alarming. It is a danger to the safety of the country, and travel near the holidays is getting harder." He said the Department of Transportation must tell people the truth.

The FAA has a new software tool called SMART that uses AI. It is part of a big plan to change air traffic control. But the company that makes SMART, Air Space Intelligence, said its product did not cause the stop.

**People waiting at airports**

The stop made a mess at airports and gave people who fly a lot of trouble. Many of them already know about late and canceled flights at holiday time.

Duffy said flights would start again, but it would take time.

Regina Harling was at the Dallas Fort Worth airport. She waited for hours before she learned about the stop from the news on her phone. "There's still a sign right in front of me that says we're boarding in 16 minutes," she said.

Harling had to fly to San Diego at the last minute because of a family problem. She did not feel good about it. "It always feels like travel gets harder than it used to be," she said.

Giliann Karon was already on a plane at Chicago O'Hare when the stop began. Her flight the day before was also late because of bad weather in New York, where she was going. "It's just like, oh, of course this is happening," she said.
