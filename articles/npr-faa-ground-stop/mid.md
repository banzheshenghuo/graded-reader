A stop on all flights in the U.S. halted air travel on Friday. It ended that night, the Federal Aviation Administration said. The stop lasted about four hours and kept thousands of planes on the ground at every U.S. airport. It was one of the biggest breaks in U.S. air travel since the September 11th attacks.

At a Friday night news meeting, the U.S. transport secretary Sean Duffy said the centers that guide planes had lost their radio and screen systems on and off from the afternoon. Duffy blamed old machines.

"We have old switches, old boxes, little metal wires from the 1980s. It's an old system that went down and went down everywhere. It was too full," he said.

But reporters asked him why those systems would give way on a normal travel day. Duffy had no clear answer.

The security secretary Markwayne Mullin spoke at the same meeting. He said there was no sign of an attack. "We have seen zero evidence this is a cyber attack," he said.

By 9 p.m., more than 8,000 flights were late on Friday and 7,000 did not fly at all, the tracking site FlightAware said. The trouble came after almost 7,000 late flights on Thursday.

The FAA said the problem hit the local centers that guide planes flying high across the country. FAA leaders did not speak at the White House and gave no more detail about what went wrong.

This was only the second time since 2001 that the whole country stopped flying. The last one, in 2023, closed all U.S. airports because a system called NOTAM, which warns pilots of danger, stopped working. People feared an attack then, but the FAA later found that outside workers had deleted key files by mistake.

Richard Blumenthal, a U.S. lawmaker from Connecticut, asked late Friday for a fast and deep study of the causes. "A radio failure this wide is very alarming," he said. He called it a danger to the safety of the country and to travel near the holidays, and said the transport department must tell people the facts.

The FAA has started using a tool called SMART that runs on AI, as part of a big plan to change air traffic control. But Air Space Intelligence, the company that makes it, quickly denied that its product was to blame. "The current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system," it said on social media.

**People stuck at airports**

The stop made a mess at airports and gave people who fly a lot of trouble. Many of them are already used to late and canceled flights at holiday time. Duffy said flights would start again, but it would take time to clear the line.

At the Dallas Fort Worth airport, Regina Harling waited for hours, long after her plane should have left, before she learned of the stop from the news on her phone. "There's still a sign right in front of me that says we're boarding in 16 minutes," she said.

Harling had to fly to San Diego at the last minute because of a family problem. Stuck at the airport, she thought about how soon she could reach her family, but she did not feel good about it. "I was just thinking to myself that it always feels like it's getting harder to travel than it used to be," she said.

At Chicago O'Hare, Giliann Karon was on board a plane when the stop was ordered. Her flight the day before was already late because of bad weather in New York, where she was going. "It's just kind of like, oh, of course this is happening," she said.
