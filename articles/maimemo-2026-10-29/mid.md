# The Six Words

Thursday arrived wearing its old uniform: swim bag by the door, purple cap on the shelf, arrangements spoken aloud at breakfast by the family's secretary, who had, in fairness, already forgiven the long weekend; the family's secretary files no grudges, only nap times, and her evening evaluation of the day is due at nine. The briefcase came back from its long weekend with exactly one thing in it, which is my bias toward light Fridays. There is a pile of review words standing between me and the new ones, one hundred and forty-nine of them, and the size of the pile frustrates the version of me that wants everything tidy, while the rest of me, the honest part, knows that a pile like this is just a tutorial in patience. The pile does not care. The pile, like the boy, simply waits to be carried; only my calendar is frustrated by it, and my calendar can wait.

Six words for today. I checked them on my phone while the porridge cooled: rate, pain, pixel, open, swing, carry. It is a strange little family of words, and it took me most of the day to notice that they were not random. They were a schedule.

Open, first, at dawn: the door of the refrigerator, at 6:40, by a small person standing on a stool he moved there himself, opening it for the milk. The refrigerator, questioned later, said it had been opened by a professional. The kitchen guard logged it, one gray pixel lighting up in the night's flat line, and for once the anomaly and the boy were the same event and neither of us minded. Pain, second, on the top gum, where a molar is arriving the slow way, slowly, with complaints. He complained about the pain the only way he knows, loudly and without paperwork, and the teething made him bite the spoon, bite the tiger's ear, bite me, gently, once, which I am told is love in this stage of the language.

Then the pool, Thursday's second arrangement, and the foreign family was back, the same one, with the same loud, friendly child, and the same warm nod between grandmothers that crosses three languages without a dictionary; foreign, but never strange. The water held its consistency, thirty-two degrees, the same temperature it has held all season, the guide held his patience, and Bobby held the wall with both hands and let go. He let go. He stood in the water for two whole seconds, a small statue of courage, and then sat down in it, delighted, and the guide's evaluation of the term so far was three words: "A swimmer now." Grandma's evaluation was longer, and had a schedule in it. His success rate with the kicking improved, his rate of swallowing pool water did not, and everyone clapped, including, from the bench, one grandmother who does not do things halfway and would complain, at home, in detail, about the temperature of the small pool, which was perfect.

Swing, after, because Thursday has two appointments now and the second one is older. The swing was free; the chain knocked its little tick at the top of the arc; and Bobby, mid-air, opened both hands off the chains for half a second, testing gravity the way he tests everything, personally. My heart rate filed a complaint. His style remains all shoulders.

Carry, at home: the swim bag, the wet towel, the toys, the plush elephant who has opinions about where he rides. I carried most of it, and he carried one thing, his choice, the elephant, holding it by one ear like a man who has learned that some things you carry matter more than others.

At dinner, the last of the six words arrived without being called. Cream, in this house, is medicine by another name: one small scoop of it, the cream that launched a hundred small negotiations, the constraint standing since October; medicine, when you think about it, with better taste than most medicine, and the one constraint in this house nobody has ever tried to negotiate. and tonight he ate it at a rate his mother would call alarming and his grandmother called healthy. He opened his mouth for the spoon before it arrived, which is trust, and he clapped when the bowl was empty, which is review. The tutorial of the evening was the fish page read twice, the same way I read my own tutorials, out loud and again; the encyclopedia has reached I, where the islands are, and my bias says islands are just fish with better public relations, which the encyclopedia, to its credit, does not argue. and Grandma says we will need a bigger bookshelf before we reach Z, and she is not asking, she is informing.

The kitchen guard closed the day the way it always does: four degrees in the purple kitchen, doors counted, everything boring, the kind of consistency you only notice when it is missing, the new battery humoring us all, one month old and already more reliable than half my week; a battery, in this house, is a small promise that keeps. My briefcase, for its part, stands by the door with tomorrow's one hard task in it, which is to stay by the door. No anomaly. The machine has no idea that the words it cannot read were the best part of my day, or that somewhere in a queue, one hundred and forty-three review words are still waiting their turn, patient as furniture.

Tomorrow is Friday, and the pile gets smaller or it doesn't; either way, Thursday did its work. The boy opened the fridge, swallowed the pool, swung mid-air, carried his elephant, and grew one molar closer to the foods he cannot have yet. Six words, all of them old friends, and the week, which had been standing still, is moving again, at its own rate, carrying what it can.
