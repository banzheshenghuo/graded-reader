# New York City Pays $10 Million to Teach Workers About AI

The state of New York has a plan: teach thousands of people how to work with AI, so they can land good jobs.

## The Plan

On Thursday, state leader Kathy Hochul announced a new center on Long Island City. A training group will run it. The goal: train up to 40,000 New Yorkers for AI-related jobs by 2032, connect them with employers, and study what works.

Money comes from the state and city, and from private companies too — including Google.org, Wells Fargo, Con Edison and Adobe.

"AI is changing our jobs and making people unsure about the future," Hochul said. "New York is making sure its workers are ready."

## Why New York?

The city's tech job market is growing fast. Between 2022 and 2025, the New York area added about 30,000 tech workers — more than the San Francisco area for the first time. Much of that growth came from banks hiring AI talent.

A leader of the program said the city alone will need "millions and millions" of workers taught to use AI by 2030.

## Who Gets Trained?

The group Pursuit has done this work before. It trains people without much money, and without university degrees. About 70% of its students are Black or Latine, half receive public help, and 40% are immigrants.

Students start with simple AI tools — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot — then learn to build small computer helpers for everyday office work.

One warning from the program's leader: "If you don't really understand AI, it can be dangerous. It can feed you wrong answers. It can be like a yes-person telling you your ideas are all great."

## Why This Time Is Different

Here is a sad fact: since ChatGPT arrived, young people with computer papers are getting fewer jobs and lower pay. The first jobs that new tech workers used to take are getting smaller in number.

In past technology waves, rich people got the training first, and the gap between rich and poor grew. This time, the program wants the opposite: poor and working-class New Yorkers go to the front of the line.

"That's where the jobs are, and that's where moving up in life happens," the leader said.
