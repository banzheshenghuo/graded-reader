# New York City Is Spending $10 Million to Train Workers for AI

New York's leader Kathy Hochul announced the AI Jobs Institute on Thursday — a Long Island City center that the charity group Pursuit will design and run. The goal: train up to 40,000 New Yorkers for AI-related jobs by 2032, connect them with employers, and produce research to guide job policy. Public money comes from state and city sources; companies including Google.org, Wells Fargo, Con Edison and Adobe will pay to run it.

"AI is changing our workforce and causing worry," Hochul said. "New York is continuing to lead the nation with policies and training to ensure workers are protected and prepared."

## A Booming Tech Market

The center opens into a tech job market that has moved toward New York. The wider city area added about 30,600 tech workers between 2022 and 2025, reaching 394,300 — passing the San Francisco area for the first time in the 13-year history of CBRE's yearly tech talent report. Much of that growth came from banks and money companies hiring AI talent, though the Bay Area still employs more AI specialists.

"We know that is going to really take a village," said Pursuit CEO Nick Simmons. The city alone, he said, will need "millions and millions" of workers upskilled in AI by 2030.

## Who Gets Trained

Pursuit, started in 2012, spent most of its history training people for software jobs. About three years ago it "took a real leap of faith" and replaced its whole course plan with a 630-hour AI course, updated every week.

The people it serves: about 70% of its students are Black or Latine, half receive government help, 60% lack a four-year paper, and 40% are immigrants. The group says its programs have added more than $1 billion in future wages for graduates.

Trainees start with the basics of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot, then learn to build AI agents that handle routine business tasks. Simmons offers one warning: "If you don't really understand it, AI can be dangerous. It can feed you false answers. It can be just a yes person telling you you have the best ideas ever."

## Why This Time Is Different

The timing matters. First jobs that once took in new tech workers are getting smaller in number: since ChatGPT arrived, graduates of computer science and similar fields have been landing fewer jobs and lower pay, and first-job ads more and more ask for skills once expected only of experienced workers.

In past waves of new technology, richer New Yorkers got training first, and the gap grew. "Something we're really thinking about this time is... making sure that this time they are able to be at the front of the line," Simmons said — the institute looks first at poorer New Yorkers.

"That's where the jobs are, and this is how people move up in life," he said.
